THOUGHT:
The paper is a short instructional note, not a research study. It computes a constant residual of 3 on five points where y = 2x + 3 and the fixed predictor is 2x, then adds the mean residual b = 3 and reports zero MSE. That result follows directly from the construction: identical residuals mean the mean residual removes them exactly. The paper says so itself, and it correctly declines to claim any predictive or generalization evidence.

The main problem is that the arithmetic is trivially true by construction, and the paper's own framing is honest about that. The grounds for a non-trivial contribution are missing. There is no new method, no real dataset, no baseline beyond the supplied predictor, no held-out evaluation, and no uncertainty analysis. The paper's stated purpose is pedagogical. Judged as a research paper, it makes at most a marginal contribution.

Specific issues I noted while reading:
- The related-work section is very long relative to a five-point calculation. Many citations (GLMs, GAMs, boosting, double/debiased ML, Stein's unbiased risk estimate, adaptive data analysis) are only loosely connected to the content and read as padding.
- Several bibliography entries have suspicious metadata. For example, [1] has page range 141–141, [4] has 791–791, [10] has 15–15, [16] has 310–310, and [20] has 299–299. These look like placeholder or truncated page numbers and need checking.
- Figure 1 caption and text say "the dashed dependency shows reuse of the original observations." That is a fair description, but the figure itself is not evaluated.
- The "Figure source provenance" appears twice, once in the body and once at the end, with some duplicated and inconsistent path references (one entry says `fig_intercept_error.jpg`, while the body refers to `figure_code/fig_intercept_error/worker.R` as the entry point).
- The mention of "Figure 2 displays exactly the three recorded MSE pairs" is accurate, but two MSE values are trivially determined by (3−b)², which the paper acknowledges.

Decision: Reject. The arithmetic is correct and honestly scoped, but there is no contribution beyond a worked illustration, and the paper does not claim otherwise. The review should be calibrated to that.
