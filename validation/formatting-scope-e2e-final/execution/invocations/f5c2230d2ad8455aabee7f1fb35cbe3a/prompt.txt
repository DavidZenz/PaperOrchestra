\begin{abstract}
This synthetic teaching note demonstrates an additive correction to a fixed predictor on five observations. For inputs $(-2,-1,0,1,2)$ and targets $y=2x+3$, the predictor $2x$ has constant residual $3$. Adding the observed mean residual, $b=3$, reduces recorded mean squared error from $9$ to $0$ on the same observations used to calculate the correction. \section{Introduction}

A constant discrepancy between observed targets and fixed predictions provides a simple setting for explaining residual correction. The historical development of least squares, examined by Plackett and Stigler, places such calculations within an established tradition of fitting numerical relationships to observations \cite{Plackett1972StudiesHistory,Stigler1981GaussInvention}. Linear prediction also has broader formulations: Makhoul's tutorial studies prediction of discrete signals and least-squares criteria \cite{Makhoul1975LinearPrediction}. The present note considers a smaller instructional problem in which the slope is supplied and only an additive correction is calculated.

Residuals make the discrepancy explicit. Work on residual examination describes graphical and numerical tools for assessing fitted analyses, while broader definitions extend residual concepts beyond linear models \cite{Anscombe1963ExaminationAnalysis,Cox1968GeneralDefinition}. Transformations provide another approach to modeling, with separate attention to normality, constant variance, and additivity \cite{Box1964AnalysisTransformations}. Here, the supplied observations require neither a transformation nor a distributional assumption to establish the numerical result: every baseline residual is exactly the same.

Specifically, the five inputs are
\[
x_i\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\},\qquad y_i=2x_i+3,
\]
and the fixed predictor returns $\widehat{y}^{(0)}_i=2x_i$. Consequently,
\[
r_i=y_i-\widehat{y}^{(0)}_i=3,\qquad
b=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}r_i=3.
\]
Adding this observed mean residual gives
$\widehat{y}^{(b)}_i=2x_i+b=y_i$ on all five observations. The experimental log records mean squared errors of $9$, $2.25$, and $0$ for the respective additions $b=0$, $1.5$, and $3$. The corresponding mean absolute errors are $3$, $1.5$, and $0$. These values describe one deterministic calculation implemented in base R.

The example isolates a common residual pattern rather than the influence of particular observations. Influence measures study how individual data points affect regression estimates, and surveys relate those measures to outliers and leverage \cite{Cook1977DetectionInfluential,Chatterjee1986InfluentialObservations}. In this construction, the slope remains fixed and all five residuals equal $3$; the calculation therefore concerns a shared additive discrepancy. Its educational purpose is consistent with attention to parsimonious models and model inadequacy \cite{Box1976ScienceStatistics}. Broader work on linear models as approximations additionally emphasizes that interpreting fitted parameters and their uncertainty requires attention to modeling assumptions \cite{Buja2019ModelsAs}; this note supplies no population-level parameter inference.

The distinction between fitting and prediction is central to interpreting the zero error. Shmueli distinguishes explanatory and predictive modeling goals \cite{Shmueli2010ExplainOr}, while cross-validatory assessment and surveys of cross-validation address prediction assessment and estimator risk \cite{Stone1974CrossvalidatoryChoice,Arlot2010SurveyCrossvalidation}. Here, the same five targets determine $b$ and assess the corrected predictions. \begin{aligned}
x&=(-2,-1,0,1,2),\\
y&=(-1,1,3,5,7),\\
\widehat{y}^{(0)}&=(-4,-2,0,2,4).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are five synthetic observations rather than an external dataset. The fixed predictor $\widehat{y}^{(0)}_i=2x_i$ supplies the slope without estimation. Subtracting its predictions from the targets gives
\[
(r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4,r_5)
=(3,3,3,3,3).
\]
Thus, the baseline underpredicts each recorded target by the same amount.

\subsection{Mean-Residual Correction and Data Dependencies}

The correction is computed directly:
\[
b=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}(y_i-2x_i)
=\frac{3+3+3+3+3}{5}=3.
\]
For each supplied observation, substitution yields
\[
\widehat{y}^{(b)}_i=2x_i+b=2x_i+3=y_i.
\]
This establishes equality on the five inputs by arithmetic. The calculation is implemented in base R, a language for data analysis and graphics \cite{Ihaka1996RLanguage}; it requires no optimizer or learned architecture.

\usepackage{amsmath}
\usetikzlibrary{arrows,calc}

\definecolor{obsfill}{HTML}{EBF1F7}
\definecolor{obsdraw}{HTML}{4C78A8}
\definecolor{basefill}{HTML}{F0F0F0}
\definecolor{basedraw}{HTML}{8C8C8C}
\definecolor{resfill}{HTML}{FDF3E7}
\definecolor{resdraw}{HTML}{E08E2B}
\definecolor{teal}{HTML}{2A9D8F}
\definecolor{corrfill}{HTML}{EEF6ED}
\definecolor{corrdraw}{HTML}{59A14F}
\definecolor{evalfill}{HTML}{F4F5F7}
\definecolor{charcoal}{HTML}{3A3A3A}
\definecolor{provenance}{HTML}{555555}

\newcommand{\moduleheader}[1]{%
  {\sffamily\fontsize{9.5}{11}\selectfont\bfseries #1}}
\newcommand{\mathtext}{\fontsize{8.5}{11}\selectfont}

\begin{document}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  box/.style={
    rectangle,
    rounded corners=3pt,
    line width=0.8pt,
    inner xsep=6pt,
    inner ysep=6pt,
    outer sep=0pt,
    align=center,
    font=\mathtext
  },
  flow/.style={
    -latex,
    line width=1pt,
    draw=charcoal,
    line cap=butt,
    line join=miter
  },
  reuse/.style={
    -latex,
    line width=0.8pt,
    dashed,
    draw=provenance,
    line cap=butt,
    line join=miter
  },
  mathlabel/.style={
    font=\fontsize{8}{10}\selectfont,
    inner sep=0pt,
    outer sep=0pt,
    fill=white
  },
  textlabel/.style={
    font=\sffamily\fontsize{8}{10}\selectfont,
    inner sep=0pt,
    outer sep=0pt,
    fill=white,
    align=center
  }
]

% White 4:3 canvas.
\path[fill=white] (0,0) rectangle (14,10.5);
\path[use as bounding box] (0,0) rectangle (14,10.5);

% Column 1: observations.
\node[
  box,
  draw=obsdraw,
  fill=obsfill,
  minimum width=4cm,
  minimum height=1.8cm
] (D1) at (2.05,8.5) {
  \moduleheader{Observations}\\[5pt]
  $x=(-2,-1,0,1,2)$\\[3pt]
  $y=2x+3=(-1,1,3,5,7)$
};

% Column 2: residuals and baseline prediction.
\node[
  box,
  draw=resdraw,
  fill=resfill,
  minimum width=3cm,
  minimum height=1.8cm
] (R) at (5.95,8.5) {
  \moduleheader{Residuals}\\[5pt]
  $r_i=y_i-2x_i$\\[3pt]
  $(3,3,3,3,3)$
};

\node[
  box,
  draw=basedraw,
  fill=basefill,
  minimum width=3cm,
  minimum height=1.8cm
] (B) at (5.95,4.8) {
  \moduleheader{Baseline predictor}\\[5pt]
  $\hat{y}_0=2x$\\[3pt]
  $(-4,-2,0,2,4)$
};

% Column 3: scalar intercept correction.
\node[
  box,
  draw=teal,
  fill=teal,
  text=white,
  minimum width=1.5cm,
  minimum height=1.8cm
] (S) at (9.4,8.5) {
  {\boldmath $b=\bar{r}=3$}
};

% Column 4: corrected prediction and evaluation.
\node[
  box,
  draw=corrdraw,
  fill=corrfill,
  minimum width=3.9cm,
  minimum height=1.8cm
] (C) at (12,4.8) {
  \moduleheader{Corrected prediction}\\[5pt]
  $\hat{y}=2x+b$\\[3pt]
  $(-1,1,3,5,7)$
};

\node[
  box,
  draw=charcoal,
  fill=evalfill,
  minimum width=4.1cm,
  minimum height=1.7cm
] (E) at (11.85,1.95) {
  \moduleheader{Evaluation on the}\\[-1pt]
  \moduleheader{same five points}\\[5pt]
  {\sffamily\fontsize{8.5}{11}\selectfont
   in-sample check; no held-out data}
};

% Target observations enter the residual calculation.
\draw[flow] (D1.east) -- (R.west)
  node[midway,above=4pt,mathlabel] {$y$};

% Predictor input: a separate orthogonal branch.
\coordinate (xexit) at ($(D1.south east)+(0,0.32)$);
\draw[flow]
  (xexit) -- (4.25,7.92) -- (4.25,4.8) -- (B.west)
  node[pos=0.5,above=4pt,mathlabel] {$x$};

% Baseline prediction enters the residual calculation.
\draw[flow] (B.north) -- (R.south)
  node[midway,right=4pt,mathlabel] {$2x$};

% The upper-flank connection leaves room for its single-line label.
\coordinate (meanstart) at ($(R.north east)+(0,-0.1)$);
\coordinate (meanend) at ($(S.north west)+(0,-0.1)$);
\draw[flow,draw=resdraw] (meanstart) -- (meanend)
  node[midway,above=4pt,textlabel] {mean over five residuals};

% Baseline prediction is carried directly into the corrected prediction.
\draw[flow] (B.east) -- (C.west)
  node[midway,above=4pt,mathlabel] {$2x$};

% Scalar correction enters the top of the corrected-prediction module.
\draw[flow,draw=teal]
  (S.east) -- (12,8.5) -- (C.north)
  node[midway,right=4pt,mathlabel] {$+b$};

% In-sample comparison.
\coordinate (evaluationentry) at (C.south |- E.north);
\draw[flow] (C.south) -- (evaluationentry)
  node[midway,left=4pt,textlabel]
  {compare $\hat{y}$ with $y$};

% Provenance: the original observations are reused for evaluation.
\coordinate (reusebottom) at ($(E.south)+(0,-0.8)$);
\draw[reuse]
  (D1.south)
  -- (D1.south |- reusebottom)
  -- (reusebottom)
  -- (E.south);

\node[
  font=\sffamily\itshape\fontsize{7.5}{9}\selectfont,
  text=provenance,
  fill=white,
  inner sep=0pt,
  anchor=south
] at ($(D1.south |- reusebottom)!0.5!(reusebottom)+(0,4pt)$)
  {same five observations reused};

\end{tikzpicture}
\end{document}
