\begin{abstract}
This teaching note connects residual arithmetic, fixed-slope intercept estimation, and training-error measurement through five synthetic observations. Targets follow $y=2x+3$, while the baseline predicts $2x$. The mean residual gives the least-squares correction $b=3$. Recorded corrections $0$, $1.5$, and $3$ yield mean squared errors $9$, $2.25$, and $0$. A squared-error decomposition explains both optimality and exact cancellation. The exposition distinguishes mean-based squared-error fitting from median-based absolute-error fitting, qualifies halfway-correction scaling, and traces how the same targets determine and evaluate the correction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This note is a short technical teaching resource for a reproducible workflow demonstration. Its audience is introductory learners familiar with averages, absolute values, and squared errors. Its contribution is an inspectable sequence connecting an observation table, an intercept-fitting identity, executable calculations, and two complementary figures.

The inputs are $x=(-2,-1,0,1,2)$, with targets $y_i=2x_i+3$ and baseline predictions $\hat y_i^{(0)}=2x_i$. Fixing the slope isolates one adjustable intercept. Every baseline residual equals $3$, making the arithmetic checkable observation by observation.

The learning objectives are to derive the squared-error-optimal correction, distinguish the objectives minimized by a mean and a median, explain error scaling in this record, and identify the targets used for fitting and scoring.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Fixed Predictors and Additive Offset Corrections}

The mathematical connection is between additive offset correction and fixed-slope intercept estimation. Writing predictions as $2x_i+b$ makes residual averaging exactly a least-squares intercept fit. Section~\ref{sec:least_squares} derives this identity and contrasts it with absolute-error minimization.

The presentation places these identities beside the complete observation record and executable arithmetic. Its teaching contribution is this explicit organization of fitting and evaluation dependencies; the mathematical claims are established within the note.

\subsection{Evaluation of Corrections Estimated from Observed Residuals}

We use \emph{training error}, or \emph{in-sample error}, for scores computed on the observations used to estimate $b$. The targets enter both residual formation and scoring. Figure~\ref{fig:residual_workflow} traces this reuse. Error on a new observation is defined separately in Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Finite Observations and Residual Correction}

A residual is target minus prediction. We distinguish the \emph{baseline residual} $r_i$ from the \emph{corrected residual} $e_i(b)$:
\[
r_i=y_i-2x_i,
\qquad
e_i(b)=y_i-(2x_i+b)=r_i-b.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:observations} supplies the complete record. Its mean baseline residual is
\[
\bar r=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}r_i=3,
\]
giving fitted predictions $\hat y_i^{\mathrm{corr}}=2x_i+\bar r$.

\subsection{Least-Squares Equivalence and Error Calculation}
\label{sec:least_squares}

The training mean squared error is
\[
L(b)=\operatorname{MSE}(b)
=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}(r_i-b)^2.
\]
Writing $r_i-b=(r_i-\bar r)+(\bar r-b)$ and using
$\sum_i(r_i-\bar r)=0$ gives
\[
L(b)=
\underbrace{\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}(r_i-\bar r)^2}_{V}
+(b-\bar r)^2.
\]
Thus $b=\bar r$ uniquely minimizes MSE, with minimum $V$. For any five nonconstant residuals, $V>0$: fitting removes their common offset but leaves their centered differences. Exact cancellation requires all residuals to coincide. Here they equal $3$, so $V=0$ and $L(3)=0$.

Absolute error has a different optimum. Order the baseline residuals as
$r_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq r_{(5)}$. Then
\[
\operatorname{MAE}(b)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}|r_i-b|
\]
is minimized at the median $r_{(3)}$. To see this, pair the extreme residuals and then the next two. Each pair's absolute-distance sum is minimized between its endpoints; both intervals contain $r_{(3)}$, which also uniquely minimizes the remaining term $|r_{(3)}-b|$. The mean and median coincide at $3$ in this record, so that correction minimizes both metrics.

For the recorded observations,
\[
e_i(b)=3-b,\qquad
\operatorname{MAE}(b)=|3-b|,\qquad
\operatorname{MSE}(b)=(3-b)^2.
\]

at each recorded correction.

Here the halfway correction $b=1.5$ halves every corrected residual relative to $b=0$, so MAE is halved and MSE is quartered. For arbitrary baseline residuals, the decomposition instead gives
\[
L(0)=V+\bar r^2,\qquad
L(\bar r/2)=V+\frac{\bar r^2}{4}.
\]
Halfway correction therefore quarters the excess MSE above $V$, rather than generally quartering total MSE. Halving MAE likewise requires separate justification; it follows here from the constant residuals.

\]
Its error is zero precisely when its baseline residual equals $3$. Training agreement and new-observation agreement therefore concern different target values.
