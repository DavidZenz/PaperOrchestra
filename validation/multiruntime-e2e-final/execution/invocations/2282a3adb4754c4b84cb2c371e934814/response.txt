The arithmetic is correct. Table 1 matches 2x and 2x+3, every residual equals 3, and the decomposition L(b) = (3−b)² is valid because the cross term vanishes when the centered residuals sum to zero. The Table 2 values (MAE 3/1.5/0, MSE 9/2.25/0) match |3−b| and (3−b)². The derivation can be checked by hand, and the centering-versus-cancellation distinction in §3.2 is a useful teaching point.

The weakness is what the calculation shows. The correction is the mean residual of the same five targets, and the reported errors are computed on those same five targets. Zero MSE at b = 3 therefore holds by construction and gives no evidence about generalization. §2.2 acknowledges this but does not follow through. The data are noise-free with constant residuals, so centering removes everything. The median also gives 3 here, so the example cannot distinguish the least-squares choice from other location estimators. The contribution is described as a "teaching organization" with no evidence of pedagogical effect, and the paper has no references. The Related Work section restates standard least squares.
