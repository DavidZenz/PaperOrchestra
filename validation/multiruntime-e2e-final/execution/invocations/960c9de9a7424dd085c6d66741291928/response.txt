I checked the arithmetic against the paper's own definitions. The inputs x = (-2,-1,0,1,2) give y = (-1,1,3,5,7) and baseline 2x = (-4,-2,0,2,4), so every baseline residual is 3. The mean residual is 3, and L(b) = (3-b)^2 gives 9, 2.25, and 0 at b = 0, 1.5, and 3. MAE(b) = |3-b| gives 3, 1.5, and 0, matching Table 2. The decomposition L(b) = V + (b - rbar)^2 with V = 0 is correct, and the claim that halfway correction quarters the excess above V (rather than total MSE) is also correct. The paper contains no numerical errors that I can find.

The concern is what the example can demonstrate. All residuals are equal, so V = 0 and the optimum is exact by construction. The mean and median coincide, which means the note cannot show that they differ. Its stated teaching goal, distinguishing the mean and median objectives, is therefore not exercised. Only three values of b are evaluated, and the figure plots those three points. The median argument is correct in outline (pairing extremes, then the middle term) but compresses the uniqueness step, which needs an explicit strict inequality. The MAE halving claim is record-specific, and the paper's wording ("halves every corrected residual") reads as more general than it is until the later caveat. Section 4.2 makes a correct definitional point about new-observation error, but it has no held-out example. The reproduction path depends on external R and TikZ files that are not in the manuscript. The related-work section cites nothing, even though least squares, median (LAD) regression, and robust estimation are all standard.

Overall, this is a correct, clearly organized teaching note, but its content is textbook-level and its synthetic example is degenerate. Its contribution is pedagogical rather than a research result, so it falls at the "marginal contribution" level. I recommend rejection from a research venue, with a note that a non-degenerate example and a fuller reproducibility account would make it a useful teaching resource.
