\begin{abstract}
This teaching note connects residual arithmetic, fixed-slope least-squares intercept estimation, and error measurement through five synthetic observations. Targets follow $y=2x+3$, while a fixed predictor returns $2x$. The mean residual gives the least-squares correction $b=3$. Recorded corrections $b=0$, $1.5$, and $3$ yield mean squared errors $9$, $2.25$, and $0$. A self-contained observation table, derivation, residual-flow diagram, and recorded-error plot support hand calculation and reproduction. The instructional focus is the distinction between estimating an offset, measuring its error on the supplying observations, and interpreting exact cancellation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An additive discrepancy provides a compact setting for learning how residuals determine an intercept. This note is intended for introductory learners who can calculate averages and squared errors and are beginning to connect these operations to least-squares estimation. Its instructional contribution is an inspectable sequence: calculate residuals, derive the offset, compare absolute and squared errors, and trace which observations supply both the correction and its evaluation.

The supplied inputs are $x=(-2,-1,0,1,2)$, with targets $y_i=2x_i+3$ and fixed predictions $\hat y_i^{(0)}=2x_i$. Every target exceeds its prediction by $3$. Keeping the slope fixed isolates the additive term and makes each computational step checkable by hand.

Three recorded offsets provide complementary checkpoints. The setting $b=0$ preserves the original discrepancy; $b=1.5$ removes half of it; and the mean residual $b=3$ cancels it. These checkpoints connect the observation-level calculation to the error identities and figures below. The contribution is this teaching organization of a finite calculation.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Fixed Predictors and Additive Offset Corrections}

The mathematical reference point is least-squares intercept estimation with a fixed slope. Writing predictions as $2x_i+b$ makes the intercept the sole adjustable quantity. Section~\ref{sec:least_squares} derives directly why minimizing squared residuals selects their mean. Thus the residual correction and the fitted fixed-slope intercept are two descriptions of the same calculation.

The presentation connects this equivalence to explicit numerical records rather than introducing a separate estimation procedure. Learners can follow the same residuals through the formula, observation table, and workflow diagram.

\subsection{Evaluation of Corrections Estimated from Observed Residuals}

The targets enter both the residual average and the error calculation. Figure~\ref{fig:residual_workflow} displays this dependency explicitly. Accordingly, every reported error describes agreement on the five observations that supplied the correction. Here the exact match is explained by their common residual, which the derivation separates from the act of minimizing squared error.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Finite Observations and Residual Correction}

Table~\ref{tab:observations} contains the complete supplied observation-level record. Index its rows by $i=1,\ldots,5$ and define
\[
r_i=y_i-2x_i,
\qquad
\bar r=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}r_i=3.
\]
The corrected predictions are $\hat y_i^{\mathrm{corr}}=2x_i+\bar r$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Complete observation record. Subtracting the baseline from each target gives residual $3$.}
    \label{tab:observations}
    \begin{tabular}{rrrr}
        \toprule
        $x_i$ & $y_i$ & Baseline $2x_i$ & Corrected $2x_i+3$ \\
        \midrule
        $-2$ & $-1$ & $-4$ & $-1$ \\
        $-1$ & $1$  & $-2$ & $1$  \\
        $0$  & $3$  & $0$  & $3$  \\
        $1$  & $5$  & $2$  & $5$  \\
        $2$  & $7$  & $4$  & $7$  \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Least-Squares Equivalence and Error Calculation}
\label{sec:least_squares}

For the five observations, the fixed-slope least-squares objective is
\[
L(b)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}(r_i-b)^2.
\]
Expanding $r_i-b=(r_i-\bar r)+(\bar r-b)$ gives
\[
L(b)=
\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}(r_i-\bar r)^2
+(b-\bar r)^2,
\]
because $\sum_i(r_i-\bar r)=0$ eliminates the cross term. The first term is independent of $b$; the second is uniquely minimized at $b=\bar r=3$. This establishes the equivalence without an optimizer.

The decomposition also distinguishes centering from cancellation: subtracting the mean leaves centered residuals $r_i-\bar r$. In this record they are all zero because every original residual equals $3$. For any evaluated offset,
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_i(b)&=y_i-(2x_i+b)=3-b,\\
\operatorname{MAE}(b)&=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}|e_i(b)|=|3-b|,\\
\operatorname{MSE}(b)&=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}e_i(b)^2=(3-b)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Deterministic Calculation and Recorded Results}

The supplied experimental record is one deterministic base R calculation. Reproduction consists of entering the $x$ and $y$ columns of Table~\ref{tab:observations}, computing \verb|r <- y - 2*x| and \verb|mean(r)|, then evaluating \verb|mean(abs(y - (2*x + b)))| and \verb|mean((y - (2*x + b))^2)| at the three recorded offsets.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Recorded errors on the observations supplying the correction.}
    \label{tab:errors}
    \begin{tabular}{rrr}
        \toprule
        Added $b$ & Mean absolute error & Mean squared error \\
        \midrule
        $0$   & $3$   & $9$    \\
        $1.5$ & $1.5$ & $2.25$ \\
        $3$   & $0$   & $0$    \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The midpoint $b=1.5$ serves as a partial-correction checkpoint. It leaves residual $1.5$ at every observation, halving MAE and quartering MSE relative to $b=0$. Its teaching role is to distinguish the linear change in absolute error from the quadratic change in squared error. The value $b=3$ follows from the residual mean and produces the corrected column in Table~\ref{tab:observations}.

\section{Conclusion}

The five-observation example links a residual average to a fixed-slope least-squares intercept through an explicit decomposition. Its midpoint checkpoint distinguishes absolute from squared error, while its workflow identifies the shared estimation and evaluation targets. The resulting zero error is an exact arithmetic consequence of the supplied constant residuals.
