I checked the arithmetic against the supplied inputs. With x = (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2) and y = 2x + 3, every residual is 3, so the mean residual is 3. The recorded errors also match: MAE and MSE are 3/9 at b = 0, 1.5/2.25 at b = 1.5, and 0/0 at b = 3. The identity L(b) = var(r) + (b - r̄)² is correct because the cross term vanishes, and Table 1's corrected column equals y. The mathematics is sound.

The weaknesses are about scope and framing. The data are noise-free with a constant residual, so the note never exercises estimator variance, bias, or generalization, and the least-squares part reduces to subtracting a constant. The zero MSE at b = 3 is in-sample by construction, since the same five points supply both the correction and its score. The paper acknowledges this in Section 2.2, but the abstract and conclusion still present the zero as a substantive "error measurement" result. The "Experiments" and "Related Work" sections contain no experiments and no cited literature, and the contribution claim ("this teaching organization") is modest. I could not inspect the figure images, only their captions and provenance files, so I did not verify Figure 1 or Figure 2 beyond their stated values. The extracted Table 1 is also hard to read because its columns run together.

As a teaching note the piece is clear and reproducible, with inline R code and a clear progression from residuals to the offset to the error metrics. As a research paper its originality and significance are low, and it does not meet the bar for publication. My recommendation is Reject, with an overall score of 3 (marginal contribution). The most useful revision would be a noisy or held-out variant plus proper citations.
