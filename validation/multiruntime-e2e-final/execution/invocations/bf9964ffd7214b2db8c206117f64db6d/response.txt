I checked the arithmetic by hand. Every residual y_i − 2x_i equals 3, so b = 3. MAE(b) = |3 − b| gives 3, 1.5, 0 and MSE(b) = (3 − b)² gives 9, 2.25, 0, which matches Table 1. The target vector (−1, 1, 3, 5, 7) and the claims that b = 1.5 halves MAE and quarters MSE are also correct. The note is internally consistent and states its scope honestly.

The problem is scope. For a fixed slope, the mean residual is the least-squares intercept, which is a textbook fact. The paper never mentions it, so it cannot be presented as a finding. Its own Section 2.2 concedes that evaluation is in-sample by construction, so the zero error at b = 3 is an identity, not evidence. There is no noise, no held-out data, no alternative estimator, and b = 1.5 is an unexplained midpoint. Section 2 is titled "Related Work" but contains no literature. Figure 2 plots three points. The reproducibility claims depend on artifacts (figure_provenance.json, checksums, render.json) and an "observation-level log" that are referenced but not part of the reviewed text. The figure provenance block appears twice, once in Section 4.2 and again after the references.

Decision: Reject. The work is accurate, but it is too trivial to count as a research contribution, even as a teaching note. Soundness is good because the claims are scoped to arithmetic and that arithmetic holds. Contribution is poor and originality is low. Quality is medium because the calculation is correct but the design is degenerate. Clarity and presentation are good. Confidence is high because the arithmetic is fully verifiable and the judgment on scope is clear.
