I checked the arithmetic directly. With x = (−2, −1, 0, 1, 2) and y = 2x + 3, the targets are (−1, 1, 3, 5, 7), the baseline residuals are all 3, and the mean residual is 3. Table 1 matches. The training MSE is (3 − b)², giving 9, 2.25, and 0 at b = 0, 1.5, and 3. The MAE is |3 − b|, giving 3, 1.5, and 0, which matches Table 2. The decomposition L(b) = V + (b − r̄)² is correct, and so is L(r̄/2) = V + r̄²/4. The median argument (pair the extremes, then the inner pair, then the middle term) is a valid lower-bound proof that the median minimizes MAE. The stated claims are therefore mathematically sound.

The problem is contribution. Every result is a standard textbook fact: the sample mean minimizes squared error and the median minimizes absolute error. The single example is degenerate. Its residuals are constant, so the mean and median coincide, V = 0, and the midpoint scaling is exact by construction. The paper acknowledges that the mean/median contrast is not exercised by this record. Originality and significance are low, and as a research-conference submission this is a marginal contribution. It may have some value as a teaching note, which the rating reflects.

The reproducibility claims (SHA256 values, render metadata, the checksum manifest) cannot be verified from the review input, so I am not counting them as evidence. Figure 2 plots only three points, with no curve of L(b), which weakens its teaching value. Figure 1 cannot be inspected from text.
