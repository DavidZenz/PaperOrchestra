THOUGHT:
The manuscript's arithmetic checks out. With x = (-2, -1, 0, 1, 2) and y = (-1, 1, 3, 5, 7), the baseline 2x leaves a residual of 3 at every point, so the mean residual is b = 3. The MAE values (3, 1.5, 0) and MSE values (9, 2.25, 0) at b = 0, 1.5, 3 follow from MAE(b) = |3 - b| and MSE(b) = (3 - b)^2, and the paper states these identities correctly. The problem is what the result is worth. The data are noise-free and the residual is constant, so the "correction" recovers the generating intercept by construction. b is chosen from the same five points that are then used to score the model, so zero error is guaranteed and is not evidence of anything. The paper says this itself, but the framing still presents a tautology as a corrective result.

The paper also misses the most useful point it could make. With the slope fixed at 2, the mean residual is exactly the least-squares intercept, because it minimises the squared error over b. The authors never state this, and they spend most of their words on tangential literature instead. Related Work cites about 40 references on clinical logistic validation, climate bias correction, SVM sigmoid calibration, neural network calibration and boosting, then concedes that none of them supplies evidence for this calculation. That is citation padding, and it makes the manuscript look like a research paper when the content is a teaching example.

The bibliography has concrete defects. Several entries have degenerate page ranges (for example [1] 141-141, [5] 147-147, [9] 15-15, [12] 316-316, [13] 461-461, [17] 320-320, [21] 23-23, [31] 370-370, [42] 120-120). Two entries list "In arXiv (Cornell University)" as the venue. Reference [18] (Geman et al., the bias/variance dilemma for neural networks) is cited as a "tutorial on nonparametric inference", which misdescribes it.

The presentation also leaks pipeline material into the manuscript. The body and the post-reference section contain figure_code paths, SHA256 hashes, a "supplied provenance manifest" and statements such as "the figure recipes are not reconstructed or embedded". Much of this duplicates Section 4.2. Figure 1 is a schematic, so the "reproducible figures" claim covers less than the abstract suggests. Figure 2 plots three points that Table 1 already gives, so it adds little.

Strengths: the arithmetic is correct and explicitly derived, the in-sample and constant-residual limitations are stated, and the prose in Sections 3 and 4 is clear. Weaknesses: no contribution beyond a worked example, circular evaluation, missing connection to least squares, decorative and partly mis-described citations, and leaked workflow metadata. I rate this as marginal, which sits at the low end of the scale: Overall 2, Originality 1, Significance 1, Contribution 1. Quality is 2 because the arithmetic is sound but the study design cannot support any generalisation. Presentation is 2 because of the citation and provenance problems. I am confident in this assessment. Decision: Reject.
