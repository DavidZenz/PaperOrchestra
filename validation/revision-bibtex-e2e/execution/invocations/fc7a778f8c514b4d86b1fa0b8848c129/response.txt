THOUGHT:
I checked the arithmetic against the observation rows. The residuals r_i = y_i - 2x_i are constant at 3, so MAE(b) = |b-3| and MSE(b) = (b-3)^2. The table values (MAE/MSE of 3/9, 1.5/2.25, and 0/0 at b = 0, 1.5, 3) are correct. Equation 1 is correct because the cross term vanishes when the centered residuals sum to zero. The identity MSE(m) - MSE(r̄) = (m-r̄)^2 for any median m also holds, and MAE(m) <= MAE(r̄) follows from m being the MAE minimizer. The even-n pairing bound and its minimizing interval [r_(k), r_(k+1)] are stated correctly.

The mathematics is sound, but the note's value is pedagogical, not novel. The mean as squared-error minimizer and the median as absolute-error minimizer are textbook facts, and the pairing argument is classical. The central example is degenerate: with all residuals equal, every location estimator coincides, so the mean and median agreement the note emphasizes tests nothing. The learning objective of distinguishing the two losses is never shown numerically. An asymmetric residual set, such as 0, 0, 0, 1, 10, would make the distinction observable.

Other specific points:
- The odd-n sentence ("for any odd sample size") gives no formula, and the displayed bound is for even n. The n = 5 uniqueness argument is loose. The clean form is Σ|b - r_(i)| >= C + |b - r_(3)| > C = Σ at b = r_(3) whenever b ≠ r_(3).
- Every error reported is in-sample ("apparent error"). The held-out protocol at x*_j is described but never run.
- The pedagogical claims (alignment with learning objectives, lower barriers for introductory readers, value of the "audit sequence") have no evaluation behind them.
- The baseline residual and corrected error use opposite sign conventions. This is consistent but adds friction.
- Efron's apparent-error analysis is about optimism of training error. The note uses it only to interpret a diagram path and never quantifies optimism.
- The figure provenance (SHA256 checksums, R and TikZ sources) is listed, but I was limited to the manuscript text and did not inspect those artifacts. I make no claim about whether they match.
- Wickham (2009) appears to be JCGS volume 19, which is dated 2010. This needs checking.

Decision: the note is correct and clear, but it makes no research contribution beyond textbook results, and it has no empirical or pedagogical validation. I'd rate it a marginal contribution (overall 3) and recommend Reject for a research venue. It could be reframed for a teaching journal with a non-degenerate example and a classroom check.
