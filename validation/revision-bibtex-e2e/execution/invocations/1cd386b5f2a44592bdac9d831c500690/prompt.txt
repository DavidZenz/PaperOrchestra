Abstract
This technical teaching note connects an additive intercept correction, its computed errors,
and the observations used to obtain them. Five synthetic observations satisfy y = 2x + 3, while
a fixed predictor returns 2x. Their residual mean is 3, so adding this constant reproduces every
supplied target. At corrections 0, 1.5, and 3, the computed mean squared errors are 9, 2.25, and
0. A squared-error decomposition explains why removing a residual mean eliminates every error
precisely when the residuals are constant. A table and two supplied figures make the calculation
and its same-sample evaluation explicit.

This note is intended for readers learning introductory regression and for a reproducible-workflow
demonstration.
Its instructional purpose is to connect three steps that can otherwise be read
separately: computing a correction from residuals, explaining its effect on an error summary, and
tracing which observations enter evaluation. The example is a worked application of established
