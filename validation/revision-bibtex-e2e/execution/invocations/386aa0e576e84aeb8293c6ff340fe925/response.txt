THOUGHT:
The arithmetic is correct. With x = -2, ..., 2 and y = 2x + 3, every baseline residual y - 2x equals 3, so the residual mean is 3. The corrected error is b - 3, which gives MAE |b - 3| and MSE (b - 3)^2. At b = 0, 1.5, and 3 these yield 3/9, 1.5/2.25, and 0/0, matching Table 1. The decomposition in Eq. (1) is a valid identity, and the claim that squared error vanishes exactly when residuals are constant holds. The stated mathematics is sound.

The problem is scope and evidential weight. The whole note is one noise-free, five-point example constructed so that the residuals are constant. The zero error after mean correction follows from that construction, so it is not an empirical finding. The same-sample dependence is named but not quantified; no held-out, noisy, or multi-predictor case is examined. The result is standard intercept algebra, and Related Work supplies context without developing any new claim. A teaching note can be useful, but this manuscript does not show a method, dataset, or result that a research venue would weigh heavily.

Specific issues I would raise:
1. MAE is reported next to MSE, but the mean does not minimize MAE in general (the median does). The note never says this, which matters for its stated objective of distinguishing minimization from elimination.
2. "Their agreement verifies the finite computation" overstates the case. Table, text, and figure all come from the same calculation, so agreement is a consistency check.
