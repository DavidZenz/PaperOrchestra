The manuscript's mathematics is correct. I checked the residual decomposition (Eq. 1; the cross term vanishes because the centered residuals sum to zero), the absolute-loss pairing argument for n=5 and the even-n bound, and the reported values. With x = (-2,...,2), y = 2x+3, and residuals all equal to 3, the corrected errors are b-3, so MAE = |b-3| and MSE = (b-3)^2. At b = 0, 1.5, and 3 these give 3/9, 1.5/2.25, and 0/0, matching Table 1.

The weakness is the contribution, not the arithmetic. Mean-as-L2-minimizer and median-as-L1-minimizer are textbook results. The worked example is noise-free with constant residuals, so mean = median = 3 by construction. It cannot show the distinction the paper says its learning objectives target, namely why the two corrections differ. "Exact agreement" is therefore trivial here. Section 4.1 describes out-of-sample assessment but reports only in-sample apparent error, which makes the "agreement" largely tautological. The figure sources and checksums are outside what I can verify from the manuscript text, and the R source stores the three coordinate pairs as literals, so it checks transcription rather than computing the errors.

Key points for the review: the proofs are sound and clearly written, but the paper offers little beyond standard results and a single degenerate example. The most useful revision would be a dataset with non-constant residuals where mean and median differ, along with an explicit out-of-sample evaluation.
